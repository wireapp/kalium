On Apple, the app takes an account's lock only while it has execution time: in the foreground, or while an `AccountExecutionHold` keeps that account. In the background, opens, logins and logouts wait for the foreground instead of taking the lock again, so iOS does not kill a suspended app that still holds it. An account the background release paused opens again on first use in the foreground.

  - Source and ABI: breaking on Apple. `CoreLogic.suspendSessionScope`, `resumeSessionScope` and `resumeSuspendedSessionScopes` are removed; `setForeground`, `releaseBackgroundAccounts` and `holdSessionScope` replace them. `AccountResourceAccess.resumeIf` and the `resumePaused` parameter of `withResumedAccess` are removed; `pause` gains an optional `shouldPause`.
  - Behavior: `setForeground` starts true. The background release pauses every account no hold keeps, all at once, so one slow drain does not delay the others.
  - Migration: call `setForeground(true)` when entering the foreground, and `setForeground(false)` then `releaseBackgroundAccounts()` when leaving it. Hold an account for work with its own execution time, such as a call or a background task, and release the hold before that time ends. An app that iOS can launch in the background sets `setForeground(false)` before anything opens an account.
